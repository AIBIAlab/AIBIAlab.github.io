1  6079 124 THE EFFECT OF CXCL12 PROCESSING ON CD34+ CELL MIGRATION IN MYELOPROLIFERATIVE NEOPLASMS. PRIMARY MYELOFIBROSIS (PMF) AND POLYCYTHEMIA VERA (PV) ARE CHRONIC MYELOPROLIFERATIVE NEOPLASMS. PMF AND, TO A LESSER DEGREE, PV ARE CHARACTERIZED BY CONSTITUTIVE MOBILIZATION OF HEMATOPOIETIC STEM CELLS (HSC) AND PROGENITOR CELLS (HPC) INTO THE PERIPHERAL BLOOD (PB). THE INTERACTION BETWEEN THE CHEMOKINE CXCL12 AND ITS RECEPTOR CXCR4 PLAYS A PIVOTAL ROLE IN DETERMINING THE TRAFFICKING OF CD34(+) CELLS BETWEEN THE BONE MARROW (BM) AND THE PB. PMF, BUT NOT PV, IS ASSOCIATED WITH DOWNREGULATION OF CXCR4 BY CD34(+) CELLS DUE TO EPIGENETIC EVENTS. THESE TRUNCATED FORMS OF CXCL12 ARE THE PRODUCT OF THE ACTION OF SEVERAL SERINE PROTEASES, INCLUDING DIPEPTIDYL PEPTIDASE-IV, NEUTROPHIL ELASTASE, MATRIX METALLOPROTEINASE-2 (MMP-2), MMP-9, AND CATHEPSIN G.
