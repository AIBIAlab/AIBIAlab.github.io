14 2252  33 EPIGENETIC MODULATION OF VISCERAL NOCICEPTION. EPIGENETICS IS A PROCESS THAT ALTERS GENE ACTIVITY OR PHENOTYPE WITHOUT ANY CHANGES IN THE UNDERLYING DNA SEQUENCE OR GENOTYPE. THESE BIOLOGICAL CHANGES MAY HAVE DELETERIOUS EFFECTS AND CAN LEAD TO VARIOUS HUMAN DISEASES. ONGOING RESEARCH IS CONTINUING TO ILLUMINATE THE ROLE OF EPIGENETICS IN A VARIETY OF PATHOPHYSIOLOGIC PROCESSES. SEVERAL CATEGORIES OF EPIGENETIC MECHANISMS HAVE BEEN STUDIED INCLUDING CHROMATIN REMODELING, DNA METHYLATION, HISTONE MODIFICATION, AND NON-CODING RNA MECHANISMS. THE UNDERLYING PATHOPHYSIOLOGY OF DISORDERS OF BRAIN-GUT INTERACTION AND STRESS-INDUCED VISCERAL PAIN ARE NOT FULLY UNDERSTOOD AND THE ROLE OF EPIGENETIC MECHANISMS IN THESE DISORDERS ARE STARTING TO BE BETTER UNDERSTOOD. 17 6252  36 THE MICROBIOME AND CANCER: IMPLICATIONS FOR ONCOLOGY NURSING SCIENCE. BACKGROUND: APPROXIMATELY 1.6 MILLION AMERICANS WERE DIAGNOSED WITH CANCER IN 2014. ALTERATIONS IN THE NORMAL FLORA OF THE GUT MAY ALSO INFLUENCE CANCER SYMPTOMS. OBJECTIVE: THE AIM OF THIS REVIEW IS TO DESCRIBE THE EMERGING ROLE FOR THE GUT MICROBIOME IN CANCER RESEARCH, ESPECIALLY THE POTENTIAL RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN THE GUT MICROBIOME AND CANCER SYMPTOMS. METHODS: EXTANT LITERATURE WAS REVIEWED AND SYNTHESIZED. RESULTS: THE MAJORITY OF STUDIES LINKING THE GUT MICROBIOTA AND CANCER ARE ANIMAL MODELS AND FOCUS ON THE RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN DYSBIOSIS AND COLORECTAL CANCER.
