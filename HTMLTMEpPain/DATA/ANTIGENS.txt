2   771  33 CD8(+) T CELL EXHAUSTION. CD8(+) T CELLS ARE IMPORTANT FOR THE PROTECTIVE IMMUNITY AGAINST INTRACELLULAR PATHOGENS AND TUMOR. IN THE CASE OF CHRONIC INFECTION OR CANCER, CD8(+) T CELLS ARE EXPOSED TO PERSISTENT ANTIGEN AND/OR INFLAMMATORY SIGNALS. THIS EXCESSIVE AMOUNT OF SIGNALS OFTEN LEADS CD8(+) T CELLS TO GRADUAL DETERIORATION OF T CELL FUNCTION, A STATE CALLED "EXHAUSTION." EXHAUSTED T CELLS ARE CHARACTERIZED BY PROGRESSIVE LOSS OF EFFECTOR FUNCTIONS (CYTOKINE PRODUCTION AND KILLING FUNCTION), EXPRESSION OF MULTIPLE INHIBITORY RECEPTORS (SUCH AS PD-1 AND LAG3), DYSREGULATED METABOLISM, POOR MEMORY RECALL RESPONSE, AND HOMEOSTATIC PROLIFERATION. THESE ALTERED FUNCTIONS ARE CLOSELY RELATED WITH ALTERED TRANSCRIPTIONAL PROGRAM AND EPIGENETIC LANDSCAPE THAT CLEARLY DISTINGUISH EXHAUSTED T CELLS FROM NORMAL EFFECTOR AND MEMORY T CELLS. T CELL EXHAUSTION IS OFTEN ASSOCIATED WITH INEFFICIENT CONTROL OF PERSISTING INFECTIONS AND CANCERS, BUT RE-INVIGORATION OF EXHAUSTED T CELLS WITH INHIBITORY RECEPTOR BLOCKADE CAN PROMOTE IMPROVED IMMUNITY AND DISEASE OUTCOME. ACCUMULATING EVIDENCES SUPPORT THE THERAPEUTIC POTENTIAL OF TARGETING EXHAUSTED T CELLS. HOWEVER, EXHAUSTED T CELLS COMPRISE HETEROGENOUS CELL POPULATION WITH DISTINCT RESPONSIVENESS TO INTERVENTION.
