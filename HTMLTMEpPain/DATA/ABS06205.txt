13 6374  46 THE ROLE OF MITOCHONDRIA IN MYOCARDIAL DAMAGE CAUSED BY ENERGY METABOLISM DISORDERS: FROM MECHANISMS TO THERAPEUTICS. MYOCARDIAL DAMAGE IS THE MOST SERIOUS PATHOLOGICAL CONSEQUENCE OF CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASES AND AN IMPORTANT REASON FOR THEIR HIGH MORTALITY. IN RECENT YEARS, BECAUSE OF THE HIGH PREVALENCE OF SYSTEMIC ENERGY METABOLISM DISORDERS (E.G., OBESITY, DIABETES MELLITUS, AND METABOLIC SYNDROME), COMPLICATIONS OF MYOCARDIAL DAMAGE CAUSED BY THESE DISORDERS HAVE ATTRACTED WIDESPREAD ATTENTION. ENERGY METABOLISM DISORDERS ARE INDEPENDENT OF TRADITIONAL INJURY-RELATED RISK FACTORS, SUCH AS ISCHEMIA, HYPOXIA, TRAUMA, AND INFECTION. AN IMBALANCE OF MYOCARDIAL METABOLIC FLEXIBILITY AND MYOCARDIAL ENERGY DEPLETION ARE USUALLY THE INITIAL CHANGES OF MYOCARDIAL INJURY CAUSED BY ENERGY METABOLISM DISORDERS, AND ABNORMAL MORPHOLOGY AND FUNCTIONAL DESTRUCTION OF THE MITOCHONDRIA ARE THEIR IMPORTANT FEATURES.
